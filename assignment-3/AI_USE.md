# AI Use Statement

This assignment was completed with Claude Code (Anthropic) as a collaborative tool. This
statement describes what the AI did, what I directed and reviewed, and where the
substantive judgment calls were made.

## What the AI helped with

- **Implementing the estimator.** The Rigobon–Sack estimators in
  `src/heteroskedasticity.py` — the three instrument sets, the IV/2SLS algebra, the
  heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors, the rank-condition bootstrap and the
  variance decomposition — were written by Claude from the two papers' equations and then
  checked line by line against them. I asked for the Monte Carlo in
  `scripts/00_test_estimator.py` before trusting any of it, on the principle that no
  number goes in the report from an estimator that has not been shown to recover a known
  answer on simulated data.
- **Data collection.** The GDELT client, the market-data downloader and the Wikipedia
  Current Events scraper were written by Claude after I specified what the analysis
  needed. It probed both APIs directly to work out what was actually usable — which is
  how the per-day article collection was found to be impractical and replaced with the
  timeline queries.
- **Live research.** This assignment concerns events that are still unfolding and that
  post-date the model's training data, so Claude used web search to establish the 2026
  chronology (the February 28 strikes, the April 8 ceasefire, the June 17 Islamabad
  Memorandum, the July 8 collapse, the September Hormuz strikes). That chronology was used
  only to *check* the NLP index against reality, never to select the event days.
- **Drafting.** The notebook narrative and this report were drafted by Claude against the
  computed tables and revised by me.

## What I directed and decided

- **The war-risk lexicon** (`src/warrisk_lexicon.py`) is purpose-built for this
  assignment, on an escalation/de-escalation axis rather than a sentiment axis. I reviewed
  every phrase and its polarity. The reasoning for not using an off-the-shelf dictionary —
  Loughran–McDonald contains neither "ceasefire" nor "airstrike", and general sentiment
  lexicons read "peace talks collapse" as positive — is mine, and follows the same
  argument I made about hawkish/dovish tone in Assignment 2.
- **The three-component design of the news index** (attention, tone shift, contest) and
  in particular the decision to discard the *direction* of news and keep only its
  magnitude. That follows directly from what the estimator needs — days of high news
  variance, not days of known sign — and it is what makes the six "Unclear" days usable
  rather than discardable.
- **The decision to change the normalising variable.** Claude produced the variance-ratio
  diagnostic; reading it and concluding that the two-year Treasury yield cannot anchor the
  system in 2026, that Brent should, and that the paper's normalisation should be reported
  alongside rather than quietly dropped, was my call. I also decided that the failed
  version belongs in the report, because the diagnostic is itself a finding.
- **The comparison-day rule.** When the first run produced comparison days *more* volatile
  than the war-news days, I judged that the paper's footnote 7 rule was failing because
  its window was a run-up to war and mine contains a running one, and directed the
  restriction to quiet-half days. This is a documented deviation from the paper, not an
  unexamined default.
- **All output was inspected before use.** I read the selected days against the known
  chronology, checked the signs and magnitudes in Tables 2 and 3 by hand, looked at each
  figure, and traced the two anomalies (Tel Aviv's 128.55 coefficient, the rank test
  rejecting nothing) to their causes rather than reporting them as findings.

## What I did not do

I did not ask the AI to invent data or to produce conclusions without the underlying
numbers. Where the results contradict the original paper — Treasury yields, break-even
inflation and the dollar all move the opposite way from 2003 — those are the estimates
the pipeline produced, reported as they came out, with the economic interpretation
argued rather than asserted. No data files are included in this repository; everything
under `data/` is regenerated by the scripts in `scripts/`.
