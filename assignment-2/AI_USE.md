# AI Use Statement

This assignment was completed with Claude Code (Anthropic) as a collaborative tool. This
statement describes what the AI did, what I directed and reviewed, and where I made the
substantive judgment calls.

## What the AI helped with

- **Data collection scripts.** Claude wrote the scraper for federalreserve.gov (statements,
  minutes, speeches, testimony, press conference transcripts) and the market-data downloader
  (FRED, Yahoo Finance), after I specified the assignment's data requirements. It found the
  URL patterns for each document type by probing the Fed's site directly, and fixed a
  directory-naming bug and a UTF-8 mojibake issue it found in its own first pass.
- **Live research.** Because this assignment concerns real, still-unfolding events (Kevin
  Warsh's swearing-in on May 22, 2026, and the September 16, 2026 FOMC decision, which had
  not happened as of the submission deadline), Claude used web search to confirm the exact
  chair-transition date and the meeting date, and to check current market-implied odds
  (CME FedWatch) as an external benchmark for the forecast.
- **Boilerplate code.** The word-list scoring function, the FinBERT batching/GPU (MPS)
  wrapper, the trading-day reaction-window logic, and the regression and plotting code in
  the notebook were drafted by Claude and then reviewed line by line.

## What I directed and decided

- **The hawkish/dovish word list** (`data/lexicons/hawkish_dovish_wordlist.csv`) is a
  custom list built for this assignment, in the spirit of Apel & Blix Grimaldi (2012) and
  Correa et al. (2021), not an off-the-shelf dictionary. I reviewed every phrase and its
  polarity tag.
- **The reaction-window convention** (same trading day, close-to-close, since statements/
  minutes/press conferences are released at 2:00 p.m. ET and speeches have no reliable
  scraped timestamp) is a documented simplification I approved after seeing the alternative
  (next-day windows for after-hours releases) would need timestamp data the Fed's speech
  pages don't consistently publish.
- **The regression specification** (indicator change ~ tone score + change in 3-month bill,
  one tone score at a time, per the assignment's Step 3) and **the forecast** (Section 5 of
  the report) reflect my own reading of the regression output and the trend chart, not a
  number the AI invented; where I use the CME FedWatch market-implied probability as a
  comparison point, that is clearly labeled as an external benchmark, not my model's output.
- **All output was inspected before use**: I read the scraped statement/minutes/speech text
  for factual accuracy (e.g., the July 2026 dissent by Hammack, Kashkari and Logan), checked
  the regression tables for sign and significance by hand, and looked at the underlying
  numbers behind every exhibit before writing the report's interpretation.

## What I did not do

I did not ask the AI to invent data, fabricate a forecast probability, or write the report's
conclusions from scratch without my review. No data files are included in this repository,
per the assignment's instructions; everything under `data/` is regenerated by the scripts in
`scripts/`.
